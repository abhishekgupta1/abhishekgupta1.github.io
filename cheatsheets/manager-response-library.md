# Manager response library cheatsheet

A one-page reference across the 15-category manager response library. For
the full worked responses, see the [complete guide](/docs/mba-skills/manager-response-library/category-1-performance-underperformance).

<a class="topic-crosslink" href="/docs/mba-skills/manager-response-library/category-1-performance-underperformance">📖 Full guide: Manager Response Library →</a>

<div class="cheat-sheet cheat-sheet--mba">

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Missing deadlines / buggy work

Separate the pattern from the incident: one missed deadline is a
conversation, a pattern is a diagnosis (skill, motivation, or unclear
expectations) before any consequence.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Formal PIP or tough review

Zero surprises by the time you're in the room — every point should already
have been raised in a prior 1-on-1. The PIP documents a pattern, it doesn't
introduce one.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Architectural disagreement (two seniors)

Make the decision criteria explicit (perf, maintainability, time-to-ship)
before re-litigating the options — most deadlocks are stuck on undefined
criteria, not genuinely equal options.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Team member challenges your authority publicly

Address the behavior privately and immediately after, not in the moment —
public confrontation invites a public power struggle.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Top performer demands promotion now

Separate "deserved" from "available" — be explicit about what's true today,
what the path looks like, and the timeline, rather than a vague maybe.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Communicating layoffs / budget cuts

Lead with facts, not euphemism. State what's happening, why, and what's next
for the people in the room — silence and spin both destroy trust faster than
bad news delivered straight.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Mandatory process change the team resists

Explain the "why" in terms of the pain it fixes, pilot it with a subset
first, and collect feedback publicly — imposed process without buy-in gets
worked around, not followed.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Pushback from CEO/VP bypassing you

Redirect future requests through you without making it personal — "happy to
prioritize this, route it through me so I can sequence it against the rest
of the team's commitments."

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Team anxious about GenAI replacing jobs

Reframe the skill: AI changes what "good" looks like (judgment, review,
prompting) rather than eliminating the role — be concrete about what's
staying valuable.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Vendor/contractor performance issues

Set explicit SLAs and KPIs up front — most vendor conflicts are actually
disputes over expectations that were never written down.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### On-call fatigue / alert overload

Fix the alert quality before you fix the schedule — a rotation with too much
noise burns people out regardless of how fair the rotation itself is.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Employee unresponsive during work hours

Ask directly, privately, and without assuming the worst before escalating —
often a personal issue or a miscommunication about expected hours, not
disengagement.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Founders'-syndrome resistance to process

Frame new structure as what lets the company keep growing without breaking,
not as bureaucracy for its own sake — tie every new process to a specific
pain it prevents.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Blameless post-mortem after an outage

Focus on system/process gaps, not who made the mistake — punishing the
person who reported the issue guarantees the next one gets hidden.

</div>

</div>
