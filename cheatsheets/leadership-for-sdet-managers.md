# Leadership for SDET managers cheatsheet

A one-page reference for SDET leadership scenarios. For the full worked
examples and interview Q&A, see the [complete guide](/docs/mba-skills/leadership-for-sdet-managers/leadership-scenarios-guide).

<a class="topic-crosslink" href="/docs/mba-skills/leadership-for-sdet-managers/leadership-scenarios-guide">📖 Full guide: Leadership for SDET Managers →</a>

<div class="cheat-sheet cheat-sheet--mba">

<div class="cheat-card">

#### STAR & SBI

**STAR** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) — structure for telling a scenario
story in an interview or review.

**SBI** (Situation, Behavior, Impact) — structure for giving feedback: name
the situation, the specific behavior, and its impact — not a character
judgment.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Situational Leadership (Hersey-Blanchard)

Match your leadership style to the engineer's competence + confidence on
*this task*, not their title: directing (low both) → coaching → supporting
→ delegating (high both).

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Servant leadership & Radical Candor

Servant leadership: your job is removing blockers so the team can do its
best work, not commanding it.

Radical Candor (Kim Scott): care personally **and** challenge directly —
either alone is either ruinous empathy or obnoxious aggression.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### RACI for cross-team scenarios

Responsible / Accountable / Consulted / Informed — name exactly one
Accountable owner per decision before a cross-team scenario stalls on "who
decides this?"

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Prioritization: Impact vs. Effort

Plot work on impact vs. effort; pair with risk-based testing (spend
automation effort where failure cost × likelihood is highest, not where
coverage is easiest).

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Flaky test culture

Diagnose whether flakiness is infra (shared state, timing) or app-under-test
issues before rewriting tests. Quarantine flaky tests visibly — don't let
them erode trust in the whole suite silently.

<span class="cheat-see">See: Worked Scenarios</span>

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### "Ship Friday, skip regression"

Quantify the specific risk being skipped (which areas, what's the blast
radius) rather than a blanket yes/no — give the business a real trade-off,
not a veto.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Low-performing senior engineer

Diagnose first: skill gap, motivation, unclear expectations, or
situational/personal. Each needs a different fix — don't default to a PIP.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Dev vs. QE cross-team friction

Almost always a structural problem (unclear ownership, misaligned
incentives) wearing a personal costume — fix the structure, not just the
relationship.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Greenfield automation culture

Start with the highest-value, highest-confidence layer (usually API/contract
tests) to build early trust, then expand — don't try to boil the ocean with
full E2E coverage on day one.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Managing up

Come with a recommendation and the trade-off already analyzed, not just a
problem — makes it easy for a VP to say yes and easy to respect your
judgment when you push back.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Reusable answer template

1. Diagnose (what's really going on, not just the symptom)
2. Frame the trade-off (what are we actually choosing between)
3. Recommend (a specific action, not "it depends")
4. State the risk of the recommendation, and how you'll monitor it

</div>

</div>
