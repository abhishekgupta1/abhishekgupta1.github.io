# Team & organizational leadership cheatsheet

A one-page reference for conflict resolution, retention, and coaching. For
the full articles, see the [complete guide](/docs/mba-skills/team-organizational-leadership/conflict-resolution-negotiation/handling-underperformance).

<a class="topic-crosslink" href="/docs/mba-skills/team-organizational-leadership/conflict-resolution-negotiation/handling-underperformance">📖 Full guide: Team & Organizational Leadership →</a>

<div class="cheat-sheet cheat-sheet--mba">

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Handling underperformance

Diagnose before you act: skill gap, will/motivation problem, unclear
expectations, or situational/personal issue — each needs a different fix.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Navigating inter-team friction

Recurring friction is almost always a structural problem (unclear
ownership, misaligned incentives, competing priorities) wearing a personal
costume.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Resolving architectural deadlocks

Most deadlocks are stuck because decision criteria were never made
explicit — not because the technical options are genuinely equal.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Reducing developer attrition

Regretted attrition is driven by a small set of addressable causes: unclear
growth, a bad manager relationship, lack of autonomy, comp falling behind
market, and burnout.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Building career progression frameworks

Define each level along a few consistent dimensions (scope, technical
depth, mentorship, communication) with observable, behavioral criteria —
not vague adjectives.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Crafting tech interview loops

Design each stage to test one clear signal relevant to on-the-job success.
Common loop: recruiter screen → technical screen (baseline ability) →
onsite (system design, collaborative coding, behavioral).

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Running 1-on-1s

The 1-on-1 belongs to the engineer, not you — they set the agenda more
often than you do.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Managing performance reviews

A good performance review contains zero surprises — every point should
already have surfaced in a prior 1-on-1.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Adapting communication tone by audience

The same message needs a different shape for each audience — confident and
simple for sales, detailed and caveated for engineering, impact-first for
execs.

</div>

<div class="cheat-card">

#### Delegating by seniority

Delegate based on the engineer's competence and confidence for *this
specific task*, not their title.

</div>

</div>
