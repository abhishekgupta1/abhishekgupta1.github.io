## 71. A team member is consistently unresponsive on Slack/Teams during core business hours without prior notice

**Core Objective**: Restore reliable availability during agreed hours without assuming bad faith before you understand the cause.

**Mental Model / Leadership Principle**: Unexplained unresponsiveness is a pattern to diagnose, not a character flaw to accuse. It might be workload, personal circumstances, a misunderstanding of what "core hours" actually requires, or genuine disengagement — each needs a different response.

**Step-by-Step Action Strategy**:
1. Name the specific pattern with dates/times, not a vague "you're often offline."
2. Ask directly and non-judgmentally what's driving it before proposing a fix.
3. Reset explicit expectations for core-hours availability and response time.
4. Agree on how they'll flag it in advance next time something legitimately requires stepping away.

**Exact Word-for-Word Script (Do's)**:
> "I've noticed you've been hard to reach during core hours a few times this month — [specific instances]. I wanted to check in and understand what's going on before assuming anything."
>
> "Whatever's behind it, let's get clear on what I actually need: a response within [X] during core hours, or a heads-up in advance if you'll be away."
>
> "If something comes up that pulls you away unexpectedly, a quick 'stepping away, back by X' message is all I need — that alone would solve most of this."

**Phrases to Avoid (Don'ts)**:
- "You need to be online and available, period." (no room for the actual cause, invites resentment or silence)
- Assuming disengagement or laziness before asking what's actually happening.
- Escalating to a formal conversation before a direct, informal one has happened first.

## 72. Addressing "meeting fatigue" and over-collaboration by shifting the engineering team toward asynchronous communication habits

**Core Objective**: Cut unnecessary synchronous time without losing the real value some meetings provide — replace habit-driven meetings with a deliberate default.

**Mental Model / Leadership Principle**: Most recurring meetings exist because canceling one feels riskier than keeping it, not because someone evaluated its ongoing value. Force that evaluation explicitly rather than letting inertia decide.

**Step-by-Step Action Strategy**:
1. Audit current recurring meetings and ask, for each, what decision or alignment it actually produces.
2. Convert status-only meetings to written async updates by default.
3. Reserve synchronous time specifically for things that need real-time discussion (ambiguous decisions, sensitive topics).
4. Trial the change for a defined period and gather feedback before making it permanent.

**Exact Word-for-Word Script (Do's)**:
> "I want to look honestly at our recurring meetings — for each one, what decision or alignment does it actually produce that couldn't happen async?"
>
> "Let's move status updates to a written async format starting this week, and keep meetings only for things that genuinely need real-time discussion."
>
> "Let's try this for a month and check in — if something's missing without the meeting, we'll bring it back, but I'd rather test the assumption than keep it by default."

**Phrases to Avoid (Don'ts)**:
- Canceling meetings unilaterally without explaining the reasoning, which reads as dismissive of whatever value people did get from them.
- Adding new async rituals on top of the old meetings instead of actually replacing them, doubling the overhead instead of reducing it.
- Treating the shift as permanent from day one without checking whether it's actually working.

## 73. Handling a situation where remote team members feel excluded from decisions made during informal in-office conversations

**Core Objective**: Close the "hallway decision" gap so remote team members aren't structurally disadvantaged in influence or information.

**Mental Model / Leadership Principle**: Informal in-office conversations will always happen — the fix isn't to eliminate them, it's to make sure nothing consequential is actually decided there without also being written down and made visible to everyone, regardless of location.

**Step-by-Step Action Strategy**:
1. Acknowledge the pattern directly rather than downplaying it as unavoidable.
2. Set an explicit norm: any decision made informally gets written down and shared before it's considered final.
3. Actively solicit remote team members' input before finalizing anything that was informally discussed in-office.
4. Check in periodically with remote team members specifically on whether this is actually working.

**Exact Word-for-Word Script (Do's)**:
> "I hear that decisions sometimes feel like they're already made by the time they reach you — that's a real gap, and I want to fix it, not just acknowledge it."
>
> "Going forward, if something gets discussed informally in the office, it's not final until it's written down and shared with everyone — I want to hold that as a hard rule, not a nice-to-have."
>
> "Before we lock this in, I want to make sure I've actually heard from everyone who's remote, not just assumed silence means agreement."

**Phrases to Avoid (Don'ts)**:
- "That's just a natural downside of being remote." (accepts the inequity instead of addressing it)
- Treating a written recap as optional or an afterthought rather than a hard requirement before any decision is final.
- Asking remote team members if they have input only after a decision is effectively already locked in.

## 74. Managing an employee who relocated to a lower cost-of-living area without notifying HR/Payroll, creating tax and legal implications

**Core Objective**: Resolve the compliance issue promptly and correctly, while treating the employee fairly and without assuming bad intent.

**Mental Model / Leadership Principle**: Most undisclosed relocations aren't malicious — they're a process gap the employee didn't realize mattered. Treat it as a compliance fix to make together, not a disciplinary matter, unless there's clear evidence of deliberate concealment.

**Step-by-Step Action Strategy**:
1. Confirm the facts directly and calmly with the employee before escalating.
2. Loop in HR/Legal/Payroll immediately, since this has real tax and compliance implications beyond your authority to resolve.
3. Communicate next steps to the employee clearly, including any compensation or policy implications.
4. Fix the underlying process gap (e.g., a clear relocation-notification policy) so it doesn't recur across the team.

**Exact Word-for-Word Script (Do's)**:
> "I wanted to check with you directly — I understand you may have relocated to [location]. Can you confirm that for me? I want to make sure we get this handled correctly on our end."
>
> "This has some real tax and legal implications, so I need to loop in HR and Payroll right away — I want you to hear that from me first, not find out through a payroll issue."
>
> "Let's make sure going forward there's a clear process for this — I don't think this was an attempt to hide anything, and I want to fix the gap so it's clear for everyone next time."

**Phrases to Avoid (Don'ts)**:
- Assuming deliberate deception without first having a direct, calm conversation to understand what happened.
- Trying to resolve the tax/legal implications yourself instead of looping in HR and Payroll immediately.
- Publicly discussing the situation with the wider team, which isn't necessary and isn't fair to the employee.

## 75. Re-engaging remote employees who have become completely isolated, silent in meetings, and disconnected from team culture

**Core Objective**: Rebuild genuine connection, not just meeting attendance — treat isolation as a serious signal that needs direct, sustained attention.

**Mental Model / Leadership Principle**: Silence in meetings is a lagging indicator — by the time it's visible there, disconnection has usually been building for a while. Address it directly and specifically, and don't mistake restored meeting participation alone for genuine re-engagement.

**Step-by-Step Action Strategy**:
1. Reach out directly and privately, naming the specific change you've observed with care, not criticism.
2. Ask open questions about what's driving the disconnection — workload, personal circumstances, feeling unseen.
3. Create small, low-stakes ways to rebuild connection (a regular 1-on-1, a specific project with visible collaboration).
4. Track re-engagement over weeks, not a single conversation, and keep checking in.

**Exact Word-for-Word Script (Do's)**:
> "I've noticed you've been quieter in meetings and less present overall the last few weeks compared to before — I wanted to check in directly, because I care about how you're doing, not just about the meetings."
>
> "What's been going on — is it workload, something personal, or something about how connected you feel to the team right now?"
>
> "Let's set up a regular time to check in, just the two of us, and let's find a project where you're working closely with someone else on the team — I want to rebuild that connection deliberately, not just hope it happens."

**Phrases to Avoid (Don'ts)**:
- "You need to speak up more in meetings" as the entire intervention, treating a symptom instead of the underlying disconnection.
- Waiting for them to raise it themselves rather than proactively reaching out.
- Assuming one good conversation has fully resolved it without continuing to check in over the following weeks.
